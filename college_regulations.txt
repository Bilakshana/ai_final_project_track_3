UNIVERSITY ACADEMIC REGULATIONS AND STUDENT HANDBOOK
=====================================================

CHAPTER 1: ADMISSION AND ENROLLMENT

1.1 Admission Requirements
Students must have a minimum GPA of 2.5 from their secondary education to be admitted.
International students must provide proof of English proficiency (IELTS score of 6.0 or higher).
All students must submit official transcripts, two letters of recommendation, and a personal statement.

1.2 Enrollment
Students must enroll in a minimum of 12 credit hours per semester to maintain full-time status.
Part-time students may enroll in fewer than 12 credit hours but are not eligible for on-campus housing.
Registration for courses opens 4 weeks before the start of each semester.

CHAPTER 2: ACADEMIC POLICIES

2.1 Attendance Policy
Students are required to attend at least 75% of all scheduled classes.
If a student's attendance falls below 75%, they may be automatically deregistered from the course.
Medical absences must be documented with a certificate from a licensed physician within 3 days.
Students with more than 3 consecutive absences must notify their academic advisor.

2.2 Grading System
A  : 90 - 100  (4.0 GPA points)
B  : 80 - 89   (3.0 GPA points)
C  : 70 - 79   (2.0 GPA points)
D  : 60 - 69   (1.0 GPA points)
F  : Below 60  (0.0 GPA points)

Students must maintain a cumulative GPA of 2.0 or above to remain in good academic standing.
A student whose GPA falls below 2.0 for two consecutive semesters will be placed on academic probation.

2.3 Examination Rules
No electronic devices are permitted during exams unless specifically approved by the instructor.
Students must present their student ID before entering the examination hall.
Cheating or plagiarism will result in automatic failure of the course and possible expulsion.
A student who misses a final exam due to illness must apply for a make-up exam within 72 hours.

CHAPTER 3: GRADUATION REQUIREMENTS

3.1 Credit Requirements
Students must complete a minimum of 120 credit hours to be eligible for graduation.
At least 40 of those credit hours must be in the student's major field of study.
A minimum of 30 credit hours must be completed at this university (transfer credits cannot substitute).

3.2 Thesis and Capstone Project
Final-year students in research programs must submit a thesis or capstone project.
The thesis must be submitted to the review committee no later than 6 weeks before the graduation ceremony.
Students must pass an oral defense of their thesis to receive final approval.

CHAPTER 4: SCHOLARSHIP AND FINANCIAL AID

4.1 Merit-Based Scholarships
Students with a GPA of 3.5 or above are eligible to apply for the Dean's Merit Scholarship.
The scholarship covers 50% of tuition fees and is renewable every semester based on academic performance.
Applications must be submitted to the Financial Aid Office by the 15th of the first month of each semester.

4.2 Need-Based Financial Aid
Students from low-income households may apply for need-based grants.
Required documents include: proof of household income, bank statements, and a completed FAFSA form.
Financial aid awards are reviewed annually and may be adjusted based on changes in financial circumstances.

4.3 Scholarships for International Students
International students with academic excellence are eligible for the Global Leader Scholarship.
This scholarship covers full tuition and provides a monthly living stipend of $500.
Applicants must have a GPA of 3.8 or above and demonstrate community leadership.

CHAPTER 5: LIBRARY AND CAMPUS FACILITIES

5.1 Library Services
The main university library is open Monday to Friday from 8:00 AM to 10:00 PM.
On weekends, the library is open from 10:00 AM to 6:00 PM.
Students may borrow up to 5 books at a time for a period of 14 days.
Overdue books are subject to a fine of $0.50 per day.
Digital library access is available 24/7 through the student portal.

5.2 Computer Labs
Computer labs are available in Building C and Building F.
Lab hours are Monday to Saturday, 7:00 AM to 9:00 PM.
Students must use their university login credentials to access lab computers.

5.3 Student Health Center
The health center is open every weekday from 9:00 AM to 5:00 PM.
All registered students are entitled to two free consultations per semester.
Prescription costs are not covered and must be paid by the student.

CHAPTER 6: STUDENT CONDUCT AND DISCIPLINE

6.1 Code of Conduct
Students are expected to behave respectfully toward faculty, staff, and fellow students.
Harassment, discrimination, or bullying of any kind will not be tolerated.
Any form of academic dishonesty is a serious offense under the university's integrity policy.

6.2 Disciplinary Procedures
Complaints must be filed in writing to the Dean of Student Affairs within 10 business days of the incident.
The disciplinary committee will review the case and issue a decision within 30 days.
Possible sanctions include: written warning, suspension, or permanent expulsion.

6.3 Appeals Process
Students have the right to appeal any disciplinary decision within 15 days of notification.
Appeals must be submitted in writing to the Provost's Office with supporting documentation.
The appeal decision is final and binding.

CHAPTER 7: COURSE ADD/DROP AND WITHDRAWAL

7.1 Add/Drop Period
Students may add or drop courses without academic penalty during the first two weeks of each semester.
After the add/drop period, a withdrawal grade (W) will appear on the student's transcript.

7.2 Official Withdrawal
To officially withdraw from a course after the add/drop period, students must submit a withdrawal form.
Withdrawals after Week 10 of the semester require approval from both the instructor and the academic advisor.
A full withdrawal from the university must be approved by the Registrar's Office.

CHAPTER 8: INTERNSHIP AND CAREER SERVICES

8.1 Internship Program
All students in their third year or above are encouraged to complete a professional internship.
Academic credit (3 credits) is awarded for approved internships of at least 200 hours.
Students must register with the Career Services Office before starting an internship.

8.2 Job Placement Support
The Career Services Office provides resume writing workshops, mock interviews, and job fairs each semester.
Job fairs are held in October and March every academic year.
Students can access job listings and internship opportunities through the university's online career portal.
